# Grievance redressal: an audit-readiness self-check

*A one-page self-assessment for a Registrar, Dean of Student Welfare, or IQAC
Coordinator. Twelve questions. Answer them honestly and the gaps name themselves.*

This is the leave-behind. Print it, send it ahead of a meeting, or work through it on the
call. It is deliberately about the institution's own process rather than about any
software, because a checklist that only one product can pass is an advertisement, and a
Registrar can tell the difference within two questions.

---

## What the regulation actually asks for

The UGC (Redressal of Grievances of Students) Regulations, 2023 require a constituted
Student Grievance Redressal Committee, resolution of grievances within a stated window,
an Ombudsperson to hear appeals, and records that survive being asked for. NAAC and NBA
visits ask to see how student grievances were handled, not merely that a committee
exists on paper.

Verify the current text and the applicable timelines against the UGC's own notification
before quoting a specific figure to a governing body. Regulations are amended, and the
number in a vendor's slide is not evidence.

---

## The twelve questions

**Constitution and roles**

1. Can you produce the current SGRC composition, with names and dates of appointment, in
   under five minutes?
2. Is an Ombudsperson appointed, and does a student know how to reach them without
   asking the office that the grievance is about?
3. Is the grievance procedure published where a student can find it without logging in?

**The record**

4. For a grievance closed eighteen months ago, can you show who handled it, what they
   decided, and when?
5. If that record were edited after the fact, would anyone be able to tell?
6. Is there one consolidated register, or does the answer depend on which office the
   grievance happened to reach?
7. What happens to the record when the person who was maintaining the spreadsheet
   leaves?

**The clock**

8. What proportion of grievances last year were resolved inside the statutory window?
   A number, not an impression.
9. When a case is about to breach that window, who finds out, and how?
10. Can you show, for any single case, the date it arrived and the date it closed,
    from a source that was not compiled after the question was asked?

**The visit**

11. How many working days did it take to assemble the grievance evidence for your last
    accreditation visit?
12. Did the last visit raise any observation about student grievance handling? If it
    did, what changed?

## Reading your answers

**Mostly confident, with documents you can actually open.** You are in better shape than
most, and you do not have a grievance problem to solve. The remaining risk is
key-person: question 7 is the one to sit with.

**Confident about the committee, vague about the record.** This is the common answer, and
it is the expensive one. The committee exists, the meetings happen, and the evidence is
reconstructed under time pressure during the weeks before a visit. That reconstruction is
the cost, and it is paid by senior staff who have other work.

**Question 5 gave you pause.** A register that can be edited without trace is not a
record, it is a document. That distinction does not usually matter until the one occasion
it matters completely: a disputed decision, a parent escalation, a legal notice.

**You could not put a number on question 8.** Then the honest position is that the
institution does not currently know whether it is compliant with the timeline, which is a
different and larger problem than being non-compliant and knowing it.

---

## What a system is for

Not to make students file more grievances. To make the ones that are filed impossible to
lose, hard to mishandle quietly, and cheap to evidence when someone asks.

If you scored well on the twelve above, you do not need one. If you did not, the useful
next step is not a demo. It is a look at your last accreditation cycle's grievance
evidence with someone who will tell you where it would not hold.
